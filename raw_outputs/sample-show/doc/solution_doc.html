What it does

Cold Open reads a show's screenplay (timestamped episode scripts) and returns a ranked launch slate: a hero promo and 4 challengers, each a 20–60 s cut that hooks in the first 3 s, ends on an unresolved beat and makes sense to a stranger. Editors review them in a web cutting room and export CMX3600 EDLs and a CSV cut list.

The rule that shapes everything: AI judges meaning, code owns facts, the editor makes the final call. The model only ever names line IDs; it never writes a timestamp, a duration or a rank.

Assumptions

  • A promo is 20–60 s (aim 30–45), built from 1–3 segments, with the hook inside the first 3.0 s.
  • The paywall sits at episode 10; a promo should land a viewer with free episodes ahead.
  • Text screenplays only: no video, music or captions. Cuts snap to shot changes in the script.
  • Meta is the primary paid channel; ad-policy risk follows Meta's advertising standards.
  • The editor has the final say; every approve, trim and reject is logged.

How it works

  • Bible. One call turns loglines, speaker labels and code-detected spelling drift into characters (with episode-scoped aliases), arcs, reveals and true continuity conflicts.
  • Tension EKG. Per-second loudness from emotion tags, punctuation, violent verbs, sound and camera cues: cheap recall, deliberately blind to meaning.
  • Mining. Per episode the miner proposes 3–6 candidates by line ID, including one quiet line away from the loud peaks; a second pass bridges moments across episodes.
  • Cold Viewer. A different model sees only what a scroller sees (faces described, never named) and says whether it would stop and what it understood; a grader checks that against the bible.
  • Slate. A transparent weighted score minus policy, spoiler, paywall and unexplained-name penalties; MMR picks a diverse slate so five promos are not five takes of one slap.

Three choices worth defending

  • IDs, not timestamps. Checked by ablation: see page 2.
  • A cold viewer from another model family, so the judge does not share the miner's blind spots or its knowledge of the plot.
  • Replayable runs. Every call is cached by content hash; a run replays offline and swapping a model is one line in config.yaml.

How we'd know it worked live

A/B the slate against editor-picked promos on equal spend: 3-second view rate, cost per install, day-1 paid conversion. Ship when editors accept 60%+ of slate promos without re-cutting.
